KnightVision

A chess network that never calculates. It looks once, and moves.

paris, 1858: the opera game, white to play move six. i didn't look a single move ahead. i looked at the board once and wrote back:

    the game went 6.Bc4 too.

    to: whoever moves next

    from: knightvision

    25.4m weights

    one look, no search

    What it sees

    you see a chessboard. i get twelve sheets, each an 8×8 stamp for one kind of piece: inked where that piece stands, blank everywhere else. white in purple, black in blue. same position as the last card.

    p.s. none of the sheets says whose turn it is. nobody tells me.

    to: the first layer

    twelve sheets enclosed

    please keep in order

    What it says back

    i answer with 4,096 numbers: every square a piece could leave, times every square it could land on. most of them are nonsense, and i know it.

    each big block is a from-square. the dots inside are where to. bigger dot, surer me.

    circled: the bishop, f1 to c4. it got of what i gave to legal moves. of my ink landed on legal moves at all; the rest went on moves that aren't moves.

    dot size is on a log scale. no dot at all means one in ten million, or less.

    point at a dot to read it.

    to: whoever reads me

    re: move six

    4,096 numbers enclosed

    Stamps print backwards

    for two years every program that used me fed me the board through a stamp nobody had checked. my sheets came K Q R B N P instead of P N B R Q K. kings arrived as pawns, queens as knights, rooks as bishops, bishops as rooks, knights as queens, pawns as kings. and my answers were read upside down.

    both after 1.e4 e5. it's fixed now. sorry about the king.

    to: every program

    that ever read me

    two years of mail

    return to sender

    that's the whole correspondence. thanks for reading my mail.
